# ORBIT Art Direction

## References to learn from
- Japanese rhythm-game graphic design: expressive typography, bold editorial layouts, strong album-art presentation.
- 1990s dance music sleeves and club flyers: geometric rhythm, halftone, sharp color contrast, controlled nostalgia.
- Modern arcade cabinets: instant hierarchy, readable feedback, tactile controls.
- Contemporary music software: precise meters, restrained technical labeling, clean alignment.

## Motifs
Use a small coherent vocabulary: optical-disc rings, orbit arcs, equalizer bars, registration marks, star/spark accents, and compact index labels. Each motif must have a job.

## Avoid
- Random HUD numbers and meaningless serial codes.
- Fake glass panels on every surface.
- Excessive bloom, lens flares, chrome, bevels, and particle spam.
- Unrelated icon styles mixed in one screen.
- AI-looking pseudo-Japanese text or illegible microcopy.
- Asset packs whose art style clashes with ORBIT.

## Asset acceptance test
An asset is accepted only if it has a clear screen/use-case, correct dimensions and transparency, a known author/source, a verified license, and a visual style that belongs in ORBIT.
